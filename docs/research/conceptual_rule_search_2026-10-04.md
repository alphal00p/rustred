## Three distinct constraints

For a source combination `C_s(n)=Σ_i w_i(n) A_{i,s}(n)` and protected axes J:

| Objective | Endpoint restriction | What it permits |
|---|---|---|
| Exact spectator preservation | `C_s=0` if any `s_j≠0` | No spectator shifts |
| Spectator no-raising | `C_s=0` if any `s_j>0` | Spectator lowering and pinches |
| Global excess nonincrease | `C_s=0` wherever `E(n+s)>E(n)` | Only a globally bounded excess envelope, if established throughout the reached program |

No-raising is **weaker**, not stronger, than exact preservation. On a negative
index, a positive physical shift reduces numerator rank; forbidding it is
still a user-selected physical-index condition, not a general rank-reduction
criterion. Restrict this nomination to the explicitly listed active spectators.
Nor does spectator no-raising prevent dot or rank growth on unprotected axes.

Polynomial vector tangency `V(D_j)=h_j D_j` is a sufficient *source-level*
construction avoiding an increase of that denominator power. It is not the
same as requiring every resulting spectator shift to be zero, nor the same
as cancellation among translated source endpoints. The proposed endpoint
test has a direct finite-column authority boundary and does not claim to
compute a complete logarithmic-derivation module.

## What the primary literature actually offers

**Forcer:** §§3.1–3.2 seek simultaneous dot/numerator lowering, then separate
boundary equations when a dot reaches1 or a numerator0. They eliminate
unwanted high-complexity terms from small translated equation sets and combine
opposing recurrences; repeated self-composition can instead inflate terms and
coefficients. This supports a constrained local elimination objective, not
unconditional whole-frontier expansion. The published triangle formula has a
massless connecting line; the diamond's lower and spectator lines must be
massless. Neither formula applies verbatim to this fully massive triangle.
[Forcer §§3.1–3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/1704.06650v1),
[diamond rule §§1–2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1504.08258).

**Smith–Zeng:** §2.3 imposes polynomial tangency on a chosen denominator set.
§3.1 distinguishes operator-level coefficient elimination from the final
small, partially symbolic neighborhood solves. Their sector definition fixes
positive powers, not only support; most indices left symbolic are numerator
indices. Consequently a successful fixed-dot rule is not automatically a rule
for arbitrary positive spectator powers. The relevant transfer is to search
only the still-unresolved boundary and retain exact lower-sector tails, not
restart unconstrained Laporta everywhere. A failed finite neighborhood here
must remain a bank/window negative, not a declaration of a new master.
[Primary paper §§2.3,3.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.11140v2).

The2026 LoopIn follow-up explicitly builds polynomial-vector identities as
combinations of differently seeded ordinary IBPs, performs coefficient-level
elimination, and subsequently solves missing rules near unresolved indices.
Its §4 warns that fewer equations are denser and rational reconstruction can
remain the bottleneck. This is compatible with our source-span architecture,
not evidence that a smaller source count or this five-loop campaign must win.
[LoopIn §§2,3.1,4](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.19909v1).

**Module intersection:** the Baikov constraints
`Σ a_i ∂_i P + bP=0` and `a_i=z_i b_i` separately avoid dimension shifts and
selected doubled denominators; intersecting their polynomial modules combines
the properties. The generators are then multiplied by monomials to supply
target relations. A cut computation loses off-cut information unless all tails
are reconstructed. A field nullspace is not a polynomial-module syzygy basis,
and the module construction is not itself a favourable-descendant certificate.
[Böhm et al., §§2.1–2.2,3](https://arxiv.org/html/1805.01873v2).

**TIDE:** the actual fully massive thesis §8.1.2 uses rank-preserving and
dot-preserving combinations only at the corresponding generated boundaries;
ordinary IBPs are often sparser elsewhere. Its larger generated halo is
explicitly distinct from the requested region, and the protected sources do
not generally eliminate the need for that halo. This is evidence against
globally replacing the ordinary generator just because a constrained class
works locally. It concerns bounded integer/one-symbolic-exponent systems,
not complete all-index rule closure. We inspected the primary thesis and
existing repository census, not an unavailable TIDE source implementation.
[Luthe thesis, pp.88–89](https://noah.nrw/ubbihs/download/pdf/5131192),
[repository comparison](tide_five_loop_census.md#6-tides-reduction-algorithm-and-what-transfers).

## Deferred stronger objective: an explicit global excess envelope

Let `E(n)=Σ max(n_i−1,0)+Σ max(−n_i,0)`. On a fixed support, it is a linear
dot-plus-rank cost; across pinches it is piecewise linear. Requiring
`E(n+s)≤E(n)` for every admitted nonzero tail, including pinches, is a different
global objective; neither it nor spectator no-raising implies the other in
general. An eventual complete-chart
predicate should use existing native sign-cell/degree geometry, not an
unproved linearization across support changes or a new polyhedral engine.

The current
`crates/rustred-core/src/foundry/artifact/source_port/total_excess.rs`
already constructs sector-dependent finite envelopes: same-support excess
does not increase, while pinch jumps use a shift-norm/support-loss bound.
The new objective would remove those jumps. Together with authenticated
nonincreasing native routing, it could provide one explicit finite physical
envelope **only if the property holds throughout the reached program**.
One improved owner or selected rule is insufficient; independently enqueued
unbounded helper domains also defeat the premise. This is not the first
conditional termination argument: finite concrete seeds, finite branching
and well-founded support/owner descent already provide one. A uniform E bound
would be quantitative and might prevent abstract widening, but its cardinality
can still be enormous. Prior bounded-helper negatives remain in force.
Reopen only on an actual offending pinch with an exactly proved replacement;
no global clipping, new scheduler or envelope implementation is proposed now.

## Next design, not a run: eliminate a costly routed boundary class

The banana results separate three objectives: few source weights, few RHS
terms, and little downstream work. None implies the others. The four-source
rule has10 tails and opens D3/D4; preserving the original numerator directions
recovers eight sources and7 tails. Its additionally allowed spectator-lowering
columns are not selected. Thus the current first-target projector is a
**feasibility selector**, not an optimizer of pinches, support sparsity or
shared descendants. Adding a permitted endpoint does not require its use.

For a fixed full source image matrix A and a prospectively chosen costly
endpoint class B, the existing exact operation is simply
`w A[:,B]=0`, with a nonzero target coefficient. Native coefficient cancellation
acts on the whole combination. The same target order and final strict
descent proof apply. This changes the admissible boundary, unlike merely
rebasing A. It can trade a costly numerator/routing branch for several easier
pinches without assigning a small cost to every pinch automatically.

This is not a new algebraic idea: Forcer's elimination of unwanted operator
terms and TIDE's wanted/unwanted boundary separation are relevant precedents
already cited above. It also uses the same F-column mechanism as our earlier
negative local-cut experiments. The prospective difference is **which complete
boundary class is excluded and why**, supported by exact weighted transport,
not a claim that a newly named constraint defeats the prior shared-work
negatives.

## Liu–Mitov: useful allowed-boundary design, not a missing solver

**Primary-source précis.** Liu–Mitov construct single-index scalar/matrix
recurrences, then a triangular alternative. Their coefficient search eliminates
unwanted integral columns while retaining a target; shifted ordinary IBPs
supply the rows. The triangular RHS lowers numerator weight, or moves within
a layer toward a chosen index hierarchy; lower-sector terms have additional
weight restrictions. Their presentation largely fixes positive indices and
omits doubled propagators, with separate base-integral treatment. Finite-basis
existence does not establish every restricted triangular ansatz; the stated
shift-depth stopping heuristic is empirical. Matrix recurrences can swell
rational coefficients. Benchmarks are one-/two-loop examples; coefficient
evaluation dominates their triangular finite-field timings, and end-to-end
comparisons are not uniformly favorable. See
[§§II.1–II.5, III.1–III.3 and IV](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05923v1).

**Repository comparison/inference.** Section III.2's forbidden-column/target
test is already the operation in
`tools/research/rule_optimizer/symbolic_projector/project.rs:343`: native
Symbolica `SparseRowReducer`, a target pivot, identity-augmented source weights
and complete image replay. The public API audit above supplies everything
needed for a finite-window test; neither a new module kernel nor a new solver
named after this paper is justified. An invertible source rebasis with the
same complete rows, columns and target cannot change exact feasibility.
Changing the *allowed RHS* can, as our locality tests demonstrate. A different
first candidate is still not evidence of an optimized boundary.

Our existing native rules already descend in their saved order. Importing a
different index hierarchy without rebuilding and proving a coherent program
would be an order change, not this experiment. Abstract containment SCCs are
also not cyclic concrete recurrences: the sealed-SCC verifier already accepts
their coverage, and the Q1707/A2738 local incoming demands were circular.
An algebraic triangular identity neither repairs that demand provenance nor
justifies clipping a broader lender. The relevant mechanism is a different
complete recurrence boundary with less downstream work, not another DAG claim.

### A distinct but presently gated pinch objective

Node2975 has physical numerator rank0 and large positive powers. A demand
that loses at least one rank unit for every support loss would exclude every
nonzero pinch there. It is not a suitable default transfer of the paper's
specialized numerator ansatz. Instead, a prospective sufficient objective is
`R(n+s) <= R(n)` for **all** retained tails, including lower-support tails,
while retaining the present saved order. At this witness it allows scalar
pinches with rank0 and excludes actual numerator-producing pinches; it does
not demand negative rank. This differs from selected-spectator no-raising and
from global dot-plus-rank E nonincrease.

The smallest useful discriminator remains gated on the evaluator's complete
selected-rule/source-cell/weighted-boundary observation. First establish an
actual surviving costly rank-increasing pinch. If none exists, this objective
does not distinguish the incumbent and should be parked. If one exists,
preregister one already justified original-source window and a complete
forbidden set for that objective, with no source/degree/order refinement.
The existing `forbidden_shifts` accepts an explicit complete set from the
actual post-fixed universe. For a rank-zero chart, strict preservation of all
initially zero indices is an available stronger sufficient restriction **only
when every original positive index is also proved unable to cross below0**.
It is not permission to ignore new numerator powers created by such crossings.
General chart-wide rank classification must use native sign/cap geometry;
singleton route observations do not supply it. No new predicate implementation
or native run is authorized here.

Require exact whole-source proof, all raw/normalization guards, all pinch
cells and saved-order descent. A finite-span miss is local; a first-candidate
proof refusal is narrower still. On success compare complete weighted routing
and eventual shared workload, including coefficient cost—not just RHS count.
This tests a particular previously allowed costly boundary, not all syzygies
or the existence of a finite master basis. Earlier boundary-polynomial and
local-cut negatives remain relevant.

### Relation to lower-sector freedom in the literature

Bree et al. explicitly identify freedom between forms equivalent under their
maximal-cut ordering but differing in lower-sector terms; optimizing those
couplings remains unimplemented in that work. This motivates investigating
boundary changes, not assuming an available optimization theorem.
[Section6](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.15381v1#S6).

The unequal-mass banana example uses a master-basis-dependent projection that
changes tadpole couplings in its differential equations. Tadpoles remain
separate master integrals; projection is not a physical proof that they vanish.
Our full-image source proof cannot import that quotient as permission to drop
pinches. [Section3.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.23594v1#S3.SS5).

Critical-syzygy construction combines generators and imposes linear vanishing
constraints on out-of-power-counting monomials. Its cut-based completeness
argument is modulo pinch integrals, with pinch topologies handled separately.
[Sections3.4 and5.1.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.17681v1#S5.SS1.SSS2).
Our smaller diagnostic instead keeps the full original-family weighted image,
all pinches and native proof conditions, while preserving the leading recurrence.
Relaxing column identity to a rank cap tests one chosen boundary objective;
